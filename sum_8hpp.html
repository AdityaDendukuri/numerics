Detailed Description

The sum of two operators, carrying whatever law both operands carry.

\((A + B)x = Ax + Bx\), evaluated without forming a matrix. The point of the type is not the arithmetic, which is two apply calls; it is that the sum keeps a law the caller already established.

The rule is a theorem, and it is exactly the meet of the two operands' laws. If \(A\) and \(B\) are both positive definite then so is \(A + B\), because \(\langle x, (A+B)x \rangle = \langle x, Ax \rangle + \langle x, Bx \rangle > 0\); the same argument gives semidefiniteness, and self-adjointness follows from linearity of the adjoint. Where the operands disagree the sum keeps only what both have: an SPD operator plus a unitary one is normal and nothing stronger.

Without this the sum would claim nothing, and a caller wanting to solve with it would have to re-assert definiteness – paying an O(n^2) probe to re-establish a fact already in hand, or asserting it unverified.
