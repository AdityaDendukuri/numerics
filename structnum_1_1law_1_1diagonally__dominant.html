Detailed Description

\(|a_{ii}| > \sum_{j \neq i} |a_{ij}|\) for every row: strict diagonal dominance.

This is the hypothesis under which Jacobi and Gauss-Seidel converge and under which Gaussian elimination needs no pivoting, so it is the precondition several routines state in prose and could not previously state in a signature.

It sits directly under endomorphism and is deliberately incomparable with the self-adjoint family: a diagonally dominant matrix need not be symmetric, and an SPD matrix need not be diagonally dominant – \(\begin{psmallmatrix} 1 & 0.9 \\ 0.9 & 1 \end{psmallmatrix}\) is SPD but not dominant. Neither implies the other, so num::law::meet_t of the two is endomorphism, and a routine that accepts either must say so with a disjunction in its requires clause rather than by naming one law. That is the difference between the ordering on laws, which is a partial order, and the logic of a constraint, which is free to use ||.
