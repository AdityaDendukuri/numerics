Estimate the extreme eigenvalues of a self-adjoint operator by power iteration.

Returns {lambda_min, lambda_max}. The maximum is found directly; the minimum comes from power-iterating the shifted operator \(\lambda_{max} I - A\), whose dominant eigenvalue is \(\lambda_{max} - \lambda_{min}\). This is what separates a definite operator from a merely semi-definite one: a null space has measure zero, so no amount of random probing will land on it.

Sampled test for skew-adjointness \(A = -A^*\), i.e. \(\mathrm{Re}\langle x, A x \rangle = 0\ \forall x\).

The comparison is relative to \(\|x\|\,\|Ax\|\) (the Cauchy-Schwarz bound on the quantity being tested), so the test does not weaken as the operator scale grows. Basis probes additionally pin every sampled diagonal entry to zero, which is what distinguishes a skew operator from a symmetric one that merely annihilates a particular probe vector.
